# Studio-light preview research and assumptions

Research checked 4 November 2026 for the separate unpublished Scenic Feature Test. All four existing Studio Lights entries are **generic classes**, not manufacturer model names. The research below informs a useful relative 2D preview; no generic item is assigned an invented lux rating, wattage, maximum throw or safety distance. This is not an exposure, rigging or electrical calculator.

## Distance, angle and light transport

[Bentham's irradiance measurement note](https://www.bentham.co.uk/knowledge/tools-resources/technical-notes/article/measurement-of-spectral-irradiance-152/) explains distance-squared falloff and the effect of incidence angle on projected area. [Edmund Optics](https://www.edmundoptics.com/knowledge-center/application-notes/lasers/an-introduction-to-optical-coatings/) explains specular reflection about the surface normal. [Westcott](https://westcottu.com/photo-reflectors-guide) distinguishes white, silver, gold, black and translucent reflector roles; [Rosco](https://us.rosco.com/en/diffusion-confusion) describes diffusion and apparent source behavior. Scenic uses these principles without claiming measured material reflectance or weave characteristics.

Newly placed Studio Lights use **Fixture-aware falloff (approx.)** in Properties. Older lights lack that optional setting and retain the legacy preview. Enabling it on an older light preserves its saved position, intensity, color, angle and throw. Disabling restores its legacy appearance. Practical lights, overhead lights and the sun remain on their previous models.

The new radial law is a softened inverse-square continuation: `core² / (core² + distance²)`. Source cores (0.5 m panel, 0.7 m softbox, 0.2 m Fresnel, 0.6 m tube) regularize near-field brightness; these are **chosen numerical stabilizers**, not measured emitter sizes. Energy is normalized to the stated relative 3 m comparison. Three transverse source samples approximate broad-source penumbra; they do not integrate a true 3D area light. Sample energies sum to the original source budget. Gaussian angular falloff reaches half peak at half the Beam angle, then tapers to zero at 1.5 times that half-angle. This angular shape is assumed, not a measured IES distribution.

**Throw / radius is the user's diagram cutoff**, not a physical maximum throw. Its last 18% fades smoothly; changing a far-away cutoff does not brighten the unchanged near field. New placement cutoffs are diagram defaults: panel 950 world units (about 26.4 ft), softbox 800 (22.2 ft), Fresnel 1400 (38.9 ft), tube 400 (11.1 ft). Existing saved cutoffs remain unchanged. Fit scene and exports include the visible traced reach when the new preview is enabled, so its light field is not cropped to the fixture icon. RGB/CCT changes tint the preview without claiming temperature-specific photometry, dimmer electronics, tungsten color shift or spectral accuracy.

Walls and indicated optical surfaces bound the paths. For the new model, projected interception already reduces received energy as the surface turns edge-on; a second arbitrary incidence penalty is not applied. Specular fronts redirect about the normal, white returns a broad field, gold warms/broadens reflection, black absorbs and silk spreads transmitted light. Secondary energies cannot exceed intercepted energy. The same three-interaction limit and maximum emitter/ray counts remain. Complex previews also limit angular drawing sectors for responsiveness; each field is first clipped to its complete traced shadow polygon, so this display simplification does not skip blockers. The map/export discloses Detail limited. Complex light-field raster layers are reused only while source/obstacle transport and canvas size/view transform match; editing, zoom, panning and exports invalidate the appropriate cache. One bounded offscreen field is retained, rather than an accumulating image cache. The opacity curve compresses dynamic range for legibility (including square-root display compression for reflected/diffused secondary fields) and is **not a lux-to-display calibration**. Ambient light is a separate artistic setting; black flags do not subtract that uniform ambient.

## Upright compact flags

Every modifier icon is shown at **78° above the floor plane**, within the requested 75–80° range. The overhead height is `physical height × cos(78°)`; width and the dashed optical-surface line stay unchanged. Actual saved dimensions, position, map rotation, front/back face and light interception do not change. Library artwork also uses a narrow upright symbol with a stand mark.

Map rotation still aims the reflective normal. The display tilt is not a vertical optical simulation. Click regions, selection outlines, marquee geometry, resizing anchors and export framing all use the same projected footprint. Corner handles scale the whole icon uniformly, preserving the current width/height ratio. Side distortion handles and independent width/height fields are disabled. Existing saved dimensions and ratios are preserved. Foreground actors/cameras retain their selection priority.

## Compatibility and limits

The optional boolean `studioOptics` is strictly validated, copied independently with objects/scenes and stored with per-shot lighting plans. Portable files/object clipboard containing it declare format 2 so public format-1 readers reject rather than discard advanced features. Earlier experimental format-2 testers may retain the field but cannot reproduce the new rendering; use the latest tester for visual round trips. No published platform's source/channel is updated by this local test.

There is no height-aware transport, calibrated lux/exposure, full global illumination, IES interpolation, measured spectral response or 3D material model. Subject and prop icons do not receive invented 3D shadows. This preview supports planning comparisons and modifier placement; on-set measurements remain necessary for real photometric decisions.
